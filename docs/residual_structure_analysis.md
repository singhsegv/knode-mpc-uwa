Generated by `evaluate/verify_residual_structure.py`.

---

## What "spread" means

**Setup:** Pick a fixed `q`. Hold it constant. Evaluate the learned residual on a grid of `(qdot, tau)` values spanning the dataset range (7×7 = 49 points). Physics says the target \(-M(q)^{-1}g(q)\) should be **identical** at every grid point.

**Spread** = how much the learned output moves across that grid:

| Metric | Formula | Observed values |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **std** (orange, top panel of summary) | \(\|\sigma(\Delta\ddot q)\|\) — RMS variation per joint, combined into one norm | ~**0.18–0.21 rad/s²** |
| **max deviation** (dashed red) | Worst grid point vs the grid mean | ~**0.34–0.43 rad/s²** |

**Want:** both ≈ 0  
**Observed:** both clearly > 0, and roughly constant over time

The network is **not** learning a pure function of `q`. It has picked up spurious dependence on `qdot` and/or `tau`.

---

## How big is that spread?

Relative to the residual magnitude (~19 rad/s²):

\[
\frac{0.18}{19} \approx 1\% \quad\text{relative variation}
\]

This is small enough that the 1D sweep plots look flat (y-axis spans ~25 rad/s²), but large enough to matter in open loop:

- Each step: wrong \(\ddot q\) by up to ~0.4 rad/s² at off-manifold \((\dot q, \tau)\)
- Over hundreds of RK4 steps, that integrates into position/velocity drift
- Drift pushes states further off the training manifold → spread error applies at worse \((\dot q, \tau)\) → compounding

Spread is **structural leakage**: velocity/torque information bleeding into a term that should be gravity-only.

## Overall conclusion

**The model learned the right gravity correction at the states it was trained on, but it also learned fake \(\dot q\)/\(\tau\) sensitivity (~1% of signal) that stays hidden on GT replay and only hurts open-loop rollouts when states drift.**

This fits the observed pattern:

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| Single-step \(\ddot q\) on GT \((q,\dot q)\) | Good |
| Open-loop rollout | States leave GT tube → wrong \(\ddot q\) → blow up |

---

## Does spread explain open-loop blow-up?

**Partially, yes** — but probably not the only factor:

| Evidence | Implication |
|----------|-------------|
| Spread ~0.2 rad/s² | Real structural bug; fix with `q`-only input |
| GT alignment perfect | Not learning the wrong gravity direction |
| Open-loop still bad | Also need longer-horizon training; spread alone is ~1% per eval |

Hard-coding \(-M^{-1}g(q)\) (zero spread by construction) should improve open loop significantly. Any remaining gap would point to integration / training horizon issues.

---

## Recommended next steps

1. **Switch residual input to `q` only** — removes the ability to learn spurious \(\dot q,\tau\) dependence; spread should drop to ~0.
2. **Re-run `verify_residual_structure.py`** after retrain — top panel of summary should hug zero.
3. **Increase training lookahead** — spread fix alone may not be enough for long open-loop rollouts.
4. **Optional sharper diagnostics:**
   - Sweep \(\dot q_2\) and \(\tau_2\) as separate panels
   - Heatmap of \(\|\Delta_\mathrm{learned} - \Delta_\mathrm{physics}\|\) over the full 2D grid

## Reference: analytic target

The imperfect nominal cancels gravity via `tau_eff = tau + g(q)`. The true residual correction is:

\[
\Delta\ddot q = -M(q)^{-1} g(q)
\]

This depends on **`q` only**, not on \(\dot q\) or \(\tau\).

See `physics_gravity_residual()` in `evaluate/verify_residual_structure.py`.
